# AI Psychosis

## Definition

**AI psychosis** is a term Karpathy jokingly uses to describe the compulsive behaviour he and other developers experience when using agentic coding tools. In March 2026 he admitted on a podcast that he hasn’t written a single line of code since December 2025. Instead, he spends up to **16 hours a day** issuing instructions to swarms of agents and orchestrating their work. His ratio of handwritten code to AI‑delegated code flipped from **80/20** to **0/100**, and he feels anxious when he has unused AI token allocations.

## Symptoms

- Continuous prompting and orchestration of multiple agents with little or no manual coding.
- Anxiety about unused tokens and a desire to maximise token throughput, sometimes called **tokenmaxxing**.
- Working extremely long hours (e.g., 16 hours per day) because agents can operate continually and the feedback loop is addictive.
- Feeling like one is no longer “programming” but expressing intent in natural language and supervising an AI team.

## Causes

The phenomenon arises from the **variable ratio reinforcement** of agentic coding tools. Each prompt can produce a successful result, delivering a dopamine hit. Failures are also stimulating because they lead to new attempts. This loop, combined with the near‑zero cost of experimentation, can make it difficult to stop prompting.

## Concerns

Karpathy and commentators warn that AI psychosis can lead to burnout, addiction and a distorted sense of productivity. Because agents often succeed at low‑hanging tasks, users may feel productive even when progress is superficial. There is a risk that **competence addiction**—feeling capable and fast—replaces genuine understanding and rest.

## Recommendations

* Recognise when you are chasing the feeling of productivity rather than accomplishing meaningful goals.
* Set boundaries on work hours and token usage to avoid burnout.
* Balance agentic workflows with periods of manual coding, deep thinking and rest.
* Use metrics beyond token throughput, such as value generated per token or time spent reviewing outputs, to assess productivity.
