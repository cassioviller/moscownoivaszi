# Slopacolypse

**Definition.** “Slopacolypse” is Andrej Karpathy’s term for the expected flood of low‑quality AI‑generated code, text and media as LLMs become ubiquitous. In his January 2026 random notes he wrote that he is “bracing for 2026 as the year of the slopacolypse across all of GitHub, Substack, arXiv, X/Instagram and generally all digital media”.

**Causes.** The barrier to producing software and content has collapsed due to agentic tools that can generate large code actions and entire articles. Many people may produce outputs without sufficient verification, leading to sloppy code, bloated abstractions and misinformation.

**Implications.**

- Expect an explosion of AI‑generated artifacts, both useful and useless. Good ideas will coexist with “AI hype productivity theater.”
- Quality control, verification and human oversight become more important than ever. Adopt **verification‑first** workflows.
- Avoid contributing to the slopacolypse by keeping code simple, specifying tasks clearly, using templates and ensuring each output has a test or metric.
- Watch for potential backlash; some commentators anticipate a backlash in 2027, although this remains speculative.

**Actions for the skill.**

- Encourage summarising and reframing rather than copying raw content.
- Use the **macro action** and **verification** templates to maintain quality.
- Track the slopacolypse’s progression in the `recent-events` and `rumor-radar` logs.
