# Decade of Agents

## Quote and Context

In June 2025, during a talk at **AI Startup School**, Karpathy cautioned against labeling 2025 “the year of agents.” Drawing on his experience at Tesla, he argued that building reliable autonomous systems requires marching through multiple **nines** of reliability and that we are at the very beginning of this process. He responded to hype by saying:

> “When I see things like ‘oh 2025 is the year of agents’ I get very concerned… **this is the decade of agents.**”

## Implications

Karpathy’s statement emphasises that the transition to agentic workflows will span years, not months. Early agents (e.g., Codex, Claude Code) are impressive but still limited: they lack full multimodality, robust memory and long‑term learning. Achieving production‑grade agent systems requires:

- **Reliability** – Like self‑driving cars, agentic systems must progress from demos to dependable products via a march of “nines.” Each additional nine (e.g., from 99 % to 99.9 % success) demands substantial engineering effort.
- **Agent‑friendly infrastructure** – Software and documentation must be re‑thought for agents. Karpathy advocates building **agent‑native interfaces** and content (e.g., LLM‑readable docs, structured APIs) rather than forcing models to click through GUIs.
- **Autonomy slider** – Borrowing from Tesla’s incremental rollout of Autopilot, he suggests creating products that span a spectrum from human‑augmented to fully autonomous modes, giving users control while gradually increasing agent independence.

## Takeaways for Skill Designers

* Think long‑term: design workflows that can grow with agent capabilities over the next decade.
* Invest in agent‑native documentation and APIs to reduce friction for agents.
* Embrace partial autonomy: combine human oversight with agent execution and allow adjustable autonomy levels.
* Prepare for continuous improvement: as agents move through successive “nines” of reliability, update programs and verification practices accordingly.
