# Source Registry

This registry lists trusted and curated sources for Andrej Karpathy’s work and public commentary. Use it to track where information originates and to justify notes and decisions.

## Official and Primary Sources

- **Karpathy’s website** – [karpathy.ai](https://karpathy.ai) – Contains his biography, blog posts and research tools such as the US Job Market Visualizer. The biography page details his career trajectory: he co‑founded OpenAI, led Tesla’s Autopilot vision team from 2017 to 2022, returned to OpenAI in 2023 to build a mid‑training and synthetic data team, taught the CS231n course at Stanford, and completed his PhD under Fei‑Fei Li.
- **GitHub repositories** – Official codebases including **micrograd** (backpropagation engine), **nanoGPT**/**nanochat** (minimal GPT training and chat harnesses), **llm.c** (LLMs in C), and **autoresearch** (agentic hyperparameter tuning). See repository READMEs and commit history for technical details.
- **Eureka Labs** – [eurekalabs.ai](https://eurekalabs.ai) – Karpathy’s AI‑native education startup. He announced in July 2024 that Eureka Labs will offer teacher‑plus‑AI courses like **LLM101n**, combining human‑curated materials with AI guidance.

## Articles and News

- **Karpathy joins Anthropic** – News articles by Axios, TechCrunch and Let’s Data Science report that on 19 May 2026 Karpathy joined Anthropic’s pre‑training team and will form a team to use Claude to accelerate research. He described himself as being in a “state of AI psychosis” and practicing “tokenmaxxing”. These sources also recap his career: OpenAI co‑founder (2015–2017), director of AI at Tesla (2017–2022), return to OpenAI (2023), founding Eureka Labs (2024) and his passion for education.
- **Karpathy’s 2025 year‑in‑review blog** – Summarises paradigm changes such as reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR), jagged intelligence, vibe coding, LLM app orchestration, and Claude Code.
- **AI Corner article “The AI Workflow Shift Explained”** – Detailed breakdown of Karpathy’s agentic workflow. Highlights include the December 2024 inflection point where he went from 80 % manual coding to 80 % agent coding, the new verb “manifest” for expressing intent, jagged intelligence, AutoResearch outperforming human intuition, token throughput as the new resource, agent personality as a product decision, documentation written for agents, Jevons paradox for software demand and the playbook for the agentic era.
- **Random notes from Claude coding** – In January 2026 Karpathy posted “A few random notes from Claude coding.” A gist of these notes captures his shift from writing code to delegating large code actions, his warnings about agents making wrong assumptions and overcomplicating code, his appreciation of agents’ tenacity and stamina, speedups and leverage, the fun of programming with agents, the atrophy of manual coding skills, the prediction of a 2026 “slopacolypse” and the questions he is asking about productivity.
- **Tokenmaxxing trend** – Articles on the rise of “tokenmaxxing” describe it as a workplace status symbol where engineers maximise their AI token consumption. Karpathy said he feels nervous when he has unused token budget and switches between Codex and Claude to exhaust his quotas. Analysis pieces caution that tokenmaxxing rewards activity over outcomes and can lead to waste and burnout.

## Additional Articles and Commentary (2025–2026)

- **“The Slopacolypse” article (Jan 2026)** – A Medium post explains Karpathy’s warning that 2026 will bring a flood of AI‑generated code and content. Karpathy revealed that about **80 %** of his coding is now performed by AI agents and that he is essentially “programming in English”. The article argues that the bottleneck has shifted from building software to **verifying** it and that traditional script‑based QA breaks when code is regenerated by agents.
- **AI psychosis articles (Mar–Apr 2026)** – Blog posts and news reports recount Karpathy’s admission that he has not written a single line of code since December 2025. He spends up to **16 hours a day** orchestrating agent swarms, flipped his handwritten‑to‑AI coding ratio from 80/20 to 0/100, and feels anxious when he has unused tokens. Karpathy jokingly calls this state **“AI psychosis”**.
- **Panews article on AI obsession (Mar 2026)** – Reports that Karpathy works 16 hours per day directing AI agents and has effectively become a manager of an AI team. The article notes that he uses an AI agent named **Dobby** to control smart‑home devices and predicts a 2026 “slopacolypse”.
- **EA Forum excerpt on self‑driving cars** – In an interview, Karpathy emphasised that self‑driving cars are still far from a solved problem. He explained that progress requires marching through successive “nines” of reliability, and that demos are misleading; each additional nine demands significant engineering effort. He cautioned that while vibe coding can tolerate errors, critical domains like self‑driving must meet extremely high reliability standards.
